Recitation 6

Taxation

Problem 1: Graphing Calculators

In the market for graphing calculators at a college town, demand and supply are given by

\( Q_D = 120 - P \) and \( Q_S = -240 + 3P \)

The calculator market is perfectly competitive, with many different producers and consumers.

(a) Equilibrium Quantity and Total Surplus

What is the equilibrium quantity of calculators sold, and what is the total surplus?

(b) Policy and Entry Effects

Next semester, the university announces that graphing calculators will be allowed on all exams. Also, Pennsylvania Devices Inc., an electronics producer, starts producing graphing calculators in December. If both of these changes happen at the same time, how will the price and quantity of graphing calculators change in equilibrium, assuming all else equal?

(c) Tax on Buyers

If the University imposes a $20 tax on the buyers of graphing calculators, how many calculators will be purchased?

(a) Equilibrium and Total Surplus

Set demand equal to supply:

\( 120 - P = -240 + 3P \Rightarrow 4P = 360 \Rightarrow P^* = 90 \)

Substitute into either equation:

\( Q^* = 120 - 90 = 30 \)

The choke prices are:

\( P_D = 120, \quad P_S = 80 \)

Total surplus is the area of the triangle:

\( TS = \frac{1}{2}(120 - 80)(30) = \frac{1}{2}(40)(30) = 600 \)

(b) Policy and Entry Effects

Allowing calculators on exams increases demand. Entry of a new producer increases supply.

\( \Rightarrow \) Quantity increases, price is ambiguous.

(c) $20 Tax on Buyers

Rewrite demand as:

\( P = 120 - Q \)

With a $20 tax:

\( P = 100 - Q \)

New demand:

\( Q = 100 - P \)

Set equal to supply:

\( 100 - P = -240 + 3P \Rightarrow 4P = 340 \Rightarrow P = 85 \)

\( Q = 100 - 85 = 15 \)

15 calculators will be purchased.
